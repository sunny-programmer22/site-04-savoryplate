Slow-Braised Tuscan Herb Chicken & Creamy Mascarpone Polenta

Tender, golden pan-seared bone-in chicken thighs gently simmered in a velvet reduction of dry Pinot Grigio, garlic confit, Kalamata olives, and sun-ripened tomatoes, presented over stone-ground polenta.

Prep Time 15 Mins
Cook Time 45 Mins
Servings 4 Portions
Calories 540 kcal
👩‍🍳 Start Cooking Guide ▶ Watch 4K Masterclass

Step-by-Step Artisan Preparation

4 Servings

Ready: Step 2 Skin Crisping
1

Season & Temper the Poultry

Pat the chicken thighs thoroughly dry with parchment paper. Moisture is the enemy of crisp skin. Season liberally with coarse kosher sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper. Allow meat to rest at room temperature for 15 minutes.

💡 Chef's Tip: Cold chicken right from the fridge causes hot oil temperature to plummet, which creates steam instead of a Maillard sear.
2

High-Heat Cast Iron Sear

Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a heavy 12-inch cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Place chicken thighs skin-side DOWN. Sear undisturbed for 6 full minutes until deep mahogany and crisp.

3

Aromatics & Deglaze with Wine

Flip chicken briefly for 2 minutes to seal the flesh side, then transfer to a plate. Drop crushed garlic cloves into the rendered fat until fragrant (60 seconds). Pour in dry Pinot Grigio, scraping up every bit of fond from the skillet bottom.

4

Gentle Low-Braised Simmer

Stir in crushed San Marzano tomatoes, sliced sun-dried tomatoes, and Kalamata olives. Nestle the chicken thighs back into the sauce, keeping skin exposed above the liquid line. Reduce heat to low, cover partially, and braise for 25 minutes.

5

Whip Polenta & Garnish

Whisk polenta into simmering chicken stock and finish with a dollop of mascarpone and aged Parmigiano Reggiano. Spoon a generous cushion of polenta onto warm plates, top with a braised thigh, spoon the glossy pan jus over, and finish with torn basil.

Chef Technique

Artisan Culinary Secrets

Fundamental techniques that elevate ordinary kitchen cooking into memorable fine dining.

🔥

The Science of Fond & Deglazing

Never wash away brown pan drippings! That caramelization contains concentrated amino acids. Deglaze while hot with acid to create restaurant pan sauces.

🍞

The Windowpane Dough Test

To verify gluten development in artisan doughs, gently stretch a small walnut-sized piece of dough. If it becomes translucent without tearing, your gluten web is set.

🧂

Layering Salt at Every Stage

Salting at the end makes food taste salty. Salting throughout cooking permeates cell walls, amplifying natural juices and sweetness without saltiness.

🧊

Batch Prep Freezing Protocols

Always flash-freeze shaped dumplings, meatballs, and fresh pasta on a flat baking sheet before bagging. Prevents sticking and preserves structural integrity.
